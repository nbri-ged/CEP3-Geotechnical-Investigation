The system strictly enforces 19 verified engineering geology and algorithmic modeling rules for stratigraphic interpolation, classification, and visualization:
Rule 1: Soil Origin Primary Partitioning
Soil layers interpolate across neighboring boreholes ONLY if they share the same depositional Origin Family. BSCS grain-size codes operate strictly within origin families.
Rule 2: Origin Family Groupings
Residual Soil & Completely Weathered Rock (CWR) form one continuous genetic in-situ weathering family. Alluvium, Colluvium, and Made Ground are treated as isolated or bounded units.
Rule 3: Transported Deposit Lens Geometry
Alluvium/Colluvium deposits with matching neighbors interpolate continuously; unmatched occurrences taper into isolated parabolic lenses capped at a 15m half-width.
Rule 4: Bedrock Weathering Grade Continuous Fade
Rock weathering grades (I to IV) render with continuous opacity fading, anchoring Fresh Bedrock (Grade I) to the solid geological lithology fill.
Rule 5: Conservative Pairing Simplification
Neighboring layers sharing Origin and BSCS codes are linked assuming deposit continuity without requiring manual deposit sequence IDs.
Rule 6: Distinct Soil Origin Hatch Patterns
Independent visual textures per origin (Alluvium waves, Colluvium triangles, Residual ticks, CWR cross-ticks) overlaid on top of BS 5930 soil color fills.
Rule 7: Contiguous Same-Origin Layer Merging
Contiguous sub-layers within one origin unit collapse into a unified deposit block keyed to the thickest representative BSCS constituent.
Rule 8: Rendered Legend Completeness Check
Dynamic legends inspect actual rendered SVG geometry to ensure every drawn soil, rock, and hatch unit is accurately represented without phantom entries.
Rule 9: Geologically-Constrained Stacking Hierarchy
Enforces natural stratigraphic superposition: Made Ground → Alluvium/Colluvium → Residual Soil → CWR → Bedrock. Surface deposits never stack below residual soil.
Rule 10: Multi-Origin Unit Internal Sub-Layering
Connected soil blocks preserve distinct grain-size sub-layer pinch-outs rather than artificially homogenizing into single-color blocks.
Rule 11: CWR Sub-Range Depth Hatch Overlay
Completely Weathered Rock depth sub-ranges are extracted from raw logging intervals and rendered with dedicated CWR texture clipped strictly to the parent unit.
Rule 12: Termination Label Stagger & Collision Avoidance
Borehole termination depth labels ('Term X.Xm') pass through a vertical collision-avoidance check that staggers overlapping text downward.
Rule 13: Full BS 5930 Soil Classification Codebook
Complete support for British Standard BS 5930 fine-grained plasticity qualifiers (CL, CI, CH, CV, CE) and coarse-grained constituents (SC, SM, GC, GM, CS, MG).
Rule 14: Lens Anchoring to Local Ground Surface
Transported lens shapes taper relative to local ground surface elevation rather than absolute datum, preventing lenses from artificially plunging into rockhead.
Rule 15: Soil Origin Boundary Transition Lines
Crisp dashed contact lines delineate genetic origin transitions (e.g. Alluvium to Residual), distinguishing genetic shifts from routine grain-size contacts.
Rule 16: Foliation Projection & Apparent Dip Engine
Apparent dip along cross-section lines is mathematically computed from true foliation strike/dip. Gneissic rock textures and formation boundaries rotate to match apparent dip.
Rule 17: Geotechnical Borehole Dashboard Multi-Column Standard
Standardized multi-column exploratory borehole schematic: Depth Scale → Core Column → Strata Descriptions → SPT N-Value Bar Chart (0-50+) → RQD % (0-100%).
Rule 18: 4-Tier Data Resilience & Offline Architecture
Multi-tiered data fetching pipeline (Live Google Sheets → CORS Proxies → Local Master CSV → Embedded Dataset) prevents map loading failure in any network state.
Rule 19: Version Tracking & Cache Purge Lifecycle
Version-driven cache invalidation clears obsolete browser storage while preserving user offline backups, coupled with automated update notifications.

Engineering & Geotechnical Disclaimer

2D geological cross-sections, foliation boundary projections, soil origin boundaries, and 3D interpolations generated by this tool are computational representations derived from discrete borehole data points. While algorithms adhere to standard BS 5930 and structural geology principles, subsurface conditions between boreholes may vary. All cross-sections and strata interpretations must be reviewed and certified by a qualified Geotechnical Engineer before final foundation, slope stability, or structural design execution.
